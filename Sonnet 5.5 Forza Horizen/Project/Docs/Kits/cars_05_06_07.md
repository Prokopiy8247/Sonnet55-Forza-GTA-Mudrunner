## 3. Recommended UE5.8 usage

* **Import**: one static mesh per FBX, `Combine Meshes` off, `Import Materials` off (assign by slot name), `Build Nanite` on for Body, Interior and
  the wheels, **off for Glass** (translucent). Scale 100 uu/m is already baked by the export recipe (X forward kept, Blender +Y -> UE -Y).
* **Collision**: Body = the three `UCX_<id>_Body_NN` hulls exported inside `<id>_Body.fbx` (use them as the vehicle's convex hull set / physics-asset
  bodies; no wheel is part of them). Wheels, Glass, Interior, SteeringWheel: no collision. Vehicle wheels: sphere/cylinder shape or Chaos wheel
  radii from the table above (GT 35/36 cm, Aether 34/36 cm, Ridge 33 cm); wheel meshes are centred on the axle so the component can drive
  the mesh directly (spin about the mesh Y axis, steer about Z, no extra offset).
* **Scale**: always uniform 1.0 (tolerance 0.98-1.02 for gameplay tuning, never non-uniform: the body, wheel arch clearances and the
  `rim_axis` vector assume 1.0).
* **Light and camera anchors** (JSON, all in Blender car-space metres, UE = x*100, -y*100, z*100):
  head L/R, tail L/R, reverse, EXHAUST L/R (VFX), hood / cockpit / bumper cameras (cockpit eye = seat centre - 0.12 m, +0.92 m, i.e. the driver's eye point;
  steering-wheel centre and axis for the driver-hand IK / wheel animation).
* **Usage / LOD**: hero player cars, garage and photo mode. Nanite handles LOD0 (126-137 k tris); only Glass (2.4-3.7 k) is a classic mesh - give it a
  cull distance of about 150 m and a 2-level LOD (50 %, 25 %) if needed. Distant LODs of the interior can simply be hidden (`Cast Shadow` off for Interior).
  Wheel meshes: 12.7-15.5 k tris each, Nanite.
* **Light bloom / emissive**: `M_Taillight` brightens on brake, `M_Reverse` on reverse, `M_Indicator` blinks, `M_Headlight` emissive in the dark; the
  glass engine hatch of the Aether is a separate glass sheet 14 mm under the paint skin, the louvres + engine are part of `<id>_Body`.

## 5. How each car was made (visual QA log, honest version)

Loop used for every car: build -> `look` (angles front-3/4, side, rear-3/4, top; material, rendered with the `qa_studio` lights, solid and wireframe for
topology; close-ups of nose, tail, wheels, flanks, interior and cockpit with `qa_focus`) -> critique -> fix -> repeat. Three to four iterations per car:

* **Meridian GT** - it1: first build read as a roadster with a hardtop (huge glass, short roof) -> redrawn side profile (fastback that keeps falling to the
  tail), thick C-pillar buttresses, black glass-house; it2: stair-stepped bumper colour split replaced by a horizontal bisect line, rolled arch lips
  (`bevel_arch_lips`), nose pockets with a bigger lift (a lens-shaped paint island was poking through the grille); it3: hood power dome, scalloped flank
  and rear haunch (`shell_deform`), mirrors moved to the door corner; it4: grilles conformed to the nose, light bar widened, cockpit camera
  re-seated at the driver's eye, sliver healing (a 1 mm boolean sliver made the body non-manifold).
* **Aether** - it1: the wing floated on hair-thin pylons and the body was 9 cm too tall -> wing re-seated to the deck (pylon feet measured on the skin),
  height 1.133 m; hex grilles poked out of the curved nose -> `conform_to_surface`; it2: nose mouth rebuilt (bars instead of bubbly hex), black valance
  line raised above the mouth (a thin paint sliver showed), door scallop and stronger shoulder line, louvres opened up so the V8 shows through the
  glass hatch, a 1 cm pocket nudge fixed a non-manifold edge at the tail extractor; it3: final conform fix (thickness kept along the projection ray, a thin
  grille bar had inverted at the nose corner), cockpit camera.
* **Ridge** - it1: first build looked like an estate (flat roof running to a vertical tail) -> roof now falls towards a 32 degree hatch glass, wing lowered
  to 1.21 m, over-sized mud flaps / tail lamps / skid plate trimmed, door number panel was a bow-tie (coarse patch cut through the door belly) ->
  dense `surface_decal`; it2: bonnet pins, body length trimmed to 4.21 m (x-scale of the shell), rear interior panel re-seated after the trim; it3: cockpit /
  bumper camera anchors, validator clean.

## 6. Known weaknesses (please read before judging the models)

* **Shared kit look**: dash, seats, door cards, wheel brake/caliper and lamp internals come from the kit's generic builders - the three cabins are
  recognisably "kit" cabins (pill-shaped dash pad, box-built seats); only the rally cabin (cage, harness, bare panel) and the Aether bulkhead differ much.
* **Glass** is a thin sheet 14 mm under the paint with a black reveal: no window seals, no chrome/rubber frames, no rear-view-mirror glass; in the Blender
  preview it is almost invisible (alpha 0.3), the cabin therefore always shows. Wiper arms are modelled as low ribs on the cowl, not articulated.
* **Aether**: scissor doors are cut lines (door groove + roof groove), not separate door meshes, hinges are not modelled; the V8 / headers / louvres are a
  stylised engine bay (about 5 k tris), the mouth / extractor grilles are shallow inserts; the wing pylons are thin swan necks; the nose and tail valances
  carry large flat black areas; lamp lenses use the kit's grey smoky `M_Headlight` (looks pale grey in Blender, fine as an emissive slot in UE).
* **Ridge**: the silhouette is a tall rally hatch and can still read as a small estate from the side; the roof scoop is a solid hump (black front face, no
  duct); cage tubes have no mounting plates, harness straps are flat boxes, number panels are flat white decals (no numerals), tyre lugs are
  regular and symmetrical (no mud, no tread wear), mud flaps and tow hooks are simple; overall length 4.21 m (> 4.15 m target) because of the
  spot-light pod and wing.
* **Meridian GT**: the greenhouse is deliberately tall-glass / low-roof - very flat roof panel; rear 2+2 bench is a kit bench; boot, engine bay and underbody are
  closed (nothing to open); the body-colour split lines (valances) are straight horizontal cuts, not moulded; hood dome is only 1.7 cm.
* **All**: bodies are single closed shells with booleans, lamp inserts and surface-hugging add-ons merged into one mesh (about 1.0-3.1 k open boundary
  edges by design: window reveals, grille cells, lens patches); there are no doors / hood / boot as separate objects, no suspension or
  wheel-well liners behind the arches (a plain cylinder with a rolled lip), no licence-plate characters (blank plates), no brand badges;
  UVs are smart-project atlases (no trim sheets / no baked AO); triangle counts sit at the upper end of the 60-150 k budget (126-137 k),
  so a hand-made LOD1 would be needed for non-Nanite platforms.
