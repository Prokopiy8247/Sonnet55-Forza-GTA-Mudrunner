# RESEARCH NOTES - Original Unity 6 Off-Road Mud Simulation

## 1. Scope, method and conventions

- **Research date:** 2026-10-16.
- **Tools:** web search and web fetch were available. Several passes ran out of search budget late (see section 8, "Unverified / gaps"). A final audit pass (section 8.1, "Verification log") re-fetched about 50 cited pages and numbers and corrected every mismatch it found; web search was exhausted by then, web fetch still worked. Also used: the offline Unity 6000.6.0f1 manual, URP 17.6.0 / Shader Graph / Test Framework 1.8.0 package source in `Library/PackageCache`, and empirical probes on this machine (Windows 11, RTX 5070, D3D12, Unity CLI 1.0.0-beta.12).
- **Purpose:** this document drives implementation of an ORIGINAL MudRunner-style off-road mud simulation in Unity 6 (URP, C#): deformable mud, project-owned suspension and tire physics, drivetrain, water, winch, trailers and cargo, a 1.5 km map, HUD and objectives.
- **Originality note:** MudRunner, Spintires, SnowRunner, Expeditions and Roadcraft were studied as REFERENCE ONLY, to learn which mechanics exist and which numbers are plausible. Nothing is copied: no code, assets, vehicle designs, maps, names or text. Real-world trucks (Ural, GAZ, Unimog, ...) appear only as scale and gearing references; our vehicles, map and branding are original. All prose here is our own wording.
- **Mapping to the brief:** section 2 = feature inventory and per-mechanic tables; section 3 = terramechanics; section 4 = Unity 6 findings and the four known pitfalls; section 5 = decisions; section 6 = committed priority list; section 7 = sources; section 8 = gaps.
- **Verdicts:** ADOPT = take the idea essentially as is. ADAPT = take the idea but change it to fit our architecture. SKIP = not for this project (reason given).
- **Evidence grades:** `[S]` read in a fetched page, paper or source file. `[D]` derived by us from sourced formulas. `[T]` our own tuning guess, to be tuned in the prototype. `[E]` measured on this machine.
- **Source codes** in tables (e.g. `GD`, `SRT`, `EXP-mud`) are resolved to URLs in section 7.
- **Reliability warning:** the only detailed public engineering account of the mud system is one developer article (`GD`, Nov 2017). The actual force equations, physics step rate, and per-wheel contact representation of the commercial games are NOT public. Everything quantitative that we build is therefore our own model, anchored on published terramechanics.

## 2. Feature inventory

### 2.1 Ground / mud deformation and interaction

| Mechanic | What it is | Verdict | Reason | Source(s) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Two-layer ground: smooth base plus separate fine mud layer | Spintires splits the level into 16 m blocks. Each has a coarse base texture (25x25 texels, ~0.67 m) and, only where mud exists, a separate 128x128 mud texture (~12.5 cm texels) storing mud height, a mud offset and a track blend factor. The 25x25 texture carries base height, a grass/dirt mix, tint and a mud-substitution alpha that changes as vehicles deform the ground. Ruts change the height of attached objects but not the base terrain height, which returns when mud is erased (BB). Roadcraft likewise stores edits as per-chunk overrides on top of base maps. | ADOPT | Exactly our plan: Unity Terrain + TerrainCollider stay smooth and static; a signed sinkage offset (negative = rut, positive = berm) lives in a sparse tile store, so reset, erase and save are deltas. Dense storage is impossible: 1536 m at 12.5 cm is ~151M cells, so only touched tiles exist. | GD, BB, RCX |
| CPU-authoritative physics data, GPU-only rendering | Each frame the CPU turns wheel and chassis position, size, velocity and penetration into "primitives" drawn into the mud texture. Physics and render data are deliberately different. The author calls the model empirical and only loosely physical; Havok could not do it out of the box. | ADOPT | Same split: CPU arrays are the truth, upload only dirty tiles, never read GPU data back into physics. | GD, 80P |
| Vertex-texture-fetch displacement of a dense grid | A plain dense vertex grid samples height and mud textures in the vertex shader; the pixel shader blends textures using normals from the height field. Batman: Arkham Origins (GDC 2014) did similar for snow with an orthographic deformation map, ping-pong accumulation and slow refill. | ADAPT | Custom URP shader on our own near-field mesh (or a forked TerrainLit); normals by central differences; fade out at window edge. Verified to compile and render in URP 17.6 (section 4.4). | GD, BAT, 80P |
| Active-tile window | 16 m blocks (SnowRunner editor: 24 m, max 84 = 2016 m). Per the MudRunner port interview, mud, water and plant physics run only in areas in front of the camera (we read this as "near the camera or truck"). Roadcraft limits deformation to the area around the player (no radius published). | ADOPT | Toroidal window of active tiles (start 5x5 to 7x7 of 16 m) around every simulated vehicle; other tiles spill to the sparse store. Window size is our `[T]`. | GD, SRED, RCD |
| Softness (tint) and wetness masks drive viscosity | Dark tint means soft ground. SnowRunner generalised it: per-surface base viscosity (MudRunner used one base viscosity for every surface), a tint brush instead of MudRunner's two tint colours, and a painted wetness mask that raises viscosity like tint does (and makes wet hard surfaces icy on winter maps). The developer post gives no numbers. | ADOPT | Visible darkness equals physical softness, so players can read terrain. Two painted masks (~0.5-1 m) scale soil stiffness and shear strength multiplicatively. | SRT, GD, RCT |
| Hidden depth floor and passability tiers | Map makers paint invisible "extrude" depth. Visible tick marks define tiers: tier 1 light vehicles, tier 2 4WD on off-road tires, beyond that only fully upgraded heavies. Hidden depth is capped on dry-looking ground for fairness (SRT). In MudRunner a second ground layer under the mud acts as a safety net when a truck sinks (BB). | ADOPT | A per-cell max sinkage depth (thickness of the soft layer above a hardpan floor) is a cheap way to author route difficulty. Map tiers to vehicle classes; cap hidden depth where the ground looks dry. | EXP-mud, SRED, SRT, RCT |
| Hidden mud revealed by wetness | "Extrudes To Wetness" sets the humidity of hidden mud (higher = more liquid; the doc gives no numeric range); a cutoff threshold removes minor mud (recommended 0.05-0.2: winter 0.2, warm seasons below 0.15, best 0.05). | ADAPT | `visibleSoftness = max(tint, hiddenDepth * smoothstep(w0, w1, wetness))`. Dynamic rain raising wetness is our own addition (originals document only painted wetness). | EXP-tp, SRED |
| Per-material malleability and borders | Soft materials (e.g. beach, dirt, forest, grass, moss, rough, soil) rut; concrete, rock_slope and us_concrete do not unless mud was painted by hand; an IsExtrudable flag decides per material, and with the default a material ruts only at full intensity (BB). Roadcraft malleability: sand = 1, asphalt = 0. Depth blends smoothly across borders. | ADOPT | `malleability` scalar 0..1 per SurfaceSO, splat-weighted. Roads and rock are 0, so the field never displaces them. | BB, RCT, SRT |
| Slip-driven digging | An extra algorithm adds mud forces when a mud layer exists or a wheel spins too fast. Spinning digs the wheel in. Heavy loads sink deeper but grip better. | ADOPT | Dig rate grows with slip speed x normal load x softness; available traction falls as sinkage grows. Gives "stop spinning" counter-play and the load trade-off without scripting. | 80P, SRT, WCC |
| Displaced volume, berms and erosion | Sumner et al.: column heightfield, displaced volume pushed to the ring outside the contact, erosion passes relax steep slopes, only active columns processed. Chrono SCM bulldozing adds erosion angle (no default), flow factor (default 1.0), erosion iterations per step (default 3) and propagation rings (default 10). | ADAPT | Per fixed step: push pressed volume beside and ahead of the footprint, then 1-3 bounded relaxation passes over the dirty rectangle with a per-soil repose angle; conserve volume approximately. | SUM, CH9 |
| Bekker / Mohr-Coulomb / Janosi on a vertical grid (Chrono SCM) | Terrain nodes move only vertically; pressure from Bekker; shear from Janosi with accumulated shear displacement; elastic unloading stiffness and vertical damping; footprint width b ~ 2A/P. | ADAPT | Use the math per tire footprint (a few dozen cells), not per-node ray-casts; closed-form per-wheel model supplies forces (section 3). | CH9, CH5, DEM |
| Wheel as cylinder; width defines the mud strip | Wheel collision is a cylinder of Radius and Width; width also defines the mud-sticking strip and track width. Tire softness = RadiusOffset (0-0.25 m) plus SoftForceScale. | ADAPT | Virtual contact patch: width = tire width, chord `2*sqrt(2rz - z^2)`. Chassis, axles and belly need their own penetration primitives so a belly-deep truck is held. | EXP-twheel, EXP-soft, GD |
| Wheel-track rendering | MudRunner renders tracks in two passes: first track heights into a separate z-projected height buffer (newer tracks pushed toward the camera, manual depth test), then the final tracks with a parallax effect; Saber uses per-tire track alpha and height textures, separate rear sets. | ADAPT | Stamp the tire's tread height into the sinkage field (real geometry and normals); use tread alpha as wetness/darkening blend. | GD, EXP-trk |
| Cosmetic spray, decals; physical mud chunks | Spray particles are cosmetic (simulated on the CPU in a separate thread; one screenshot shows about 14,000 sub-particles, spawned in chunks of 16); z-projected box decals; mud chunks are real rigid bodies that break up. | ADAPT | Spray and clods via VFX driven by slip; dirt mask on the vehicle. SKIP physical chunks for now. Decal Projector only for rare marks (decals cannot use SRP Batcher). | GD |
| Authoring brushes | Automatic (visible mud; brush size, which is a diameter, up to 2.5 m carves ruts that are lower in the middle and slightly raised at the edges; above that it acts like Extrudes plus visible mud; 0.5-0.8 m recommended for tire traces, value 0.54-0.56 light and 0.6-0.7 deep; an Autofade option eases rut ends in) vs Extrudes (hidden depth only, shown only when the ground is wet, the biggest effect on passability); QuickMud paints ruts, friability and wetness in one stroke; mud kept in its own sparse file, not backed up automatically by the editor. | ADOPT | Unity editor tools painting into the tile store; keep the sinkage asset separate from TerrainData so it can be regenerated and backed up. | EXP-mud, EXP-qmud, BB, SRED |
| Persistence and settling | Roadcraft: save size grew with every deformation; sand piles and dig depth capped; optimisation took ~6 months. Batman snow refills by subtracting a small value per frame. No public rule for mud recovery in the racing games. | ADAPT | Persist touched tiles only (16-bit deltas, drop sub-threshold values, cap depth and berm height). Optional slow settle pass on inactive recent tiles, rate per soil. | RCD, BAT, GD |
| Multi-pass compaction and remolding | Bevameter: Bekker n rises with passes in clay-loam; military cone index uses a remolding index RI for strength lost under traffic. | ADAPT | Per-cell strength scalar S. Saturated mud loses strength toward RI per heavy pass; sand and loam gain stiffness. Sign differs by soil. Only the trend is relied on. | ASD, GEO |
| Viscous drag in deep mud | Mud and clay slurries behave as Bingham plastics (yield stress, then viscous flow); classical Bekker has no velocity dependence. | ADAPT | Velocity-dependent drag on submerged wheel, axle and body volume in deep mud and bog; yield term tied to cohesion. Pure tuning `[T]`. | WBP |
| Static props and trees vs the field | Static models sink only partly into mud and get no buoyancy or water drag; plants raise terrain slightly. | ADAPT | Decide explicitly: props are static colliders with no sinkage interaction; trailers and cargo are rigid bodies with their own simple contact patches. | BB |
| Readable cues and terrain honesty | Dark soil is soft; reeds on banks; Hardcore lets hazardous routes look safe. | ADOPT / ADAPT | Cue language: dark wet ground, puddles, reeds, ruts. A "terrain honesty" setting: Normal keeps hidden depth correlated with visuals; Hardcore allows some deceptive pockets. | PSB, LUDO-map, MM347 |
| Fixed-rate custom ground step | The mud model is custom code on top of a physics engine; step rate unknown. | ADOPT | All ground code in FixedUpdate at 100 Hz, deterministic order: suspension -> patch sampling -> forces -> sinkage write-back. | GD |
| Snow depth layer; layer-count limits | SnowRunner paints snow depth (1 px = 50 cm); its materials are limited to 4 layers; Roadcraft handled at most 2 materials per area well. | SKIP | No snow biome. Budget 4 splat layers per tile or use a Texture2DArray; engine-specific limits do not apply. | EXP-snow, SRED, RCD |

### 2.4 Water

| Mechanic | What it is | Verdict | Reason | Source(s) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Intake / snorkel drowning point | A vehicle has an intake (or snorkel) point; water above it causes stall and damage. MudRunner stalls and damages a flooded engine. SnowRunner patch notes show intake height and damage often disagreed. | ADOPT | One canonical `intakeLocalPoint` per vehicle (snorkel overrides), tested against water height; grace ~0.5 s, then stall, engine damage, restart only when clear. | Rivers as point chains with flow | Each river point carries a water height (its Y position) and a width; flow follows point order (invertible) or a painted flow map; the water brush also paints speed (blue channel, 1 = maximum) and foam (green channel); by default shallower water flows slower with smaller waves. | Multi-point buoyancy | BoatAttack's buoyancy script has a one-point mode and a voxel mode: the voxel mode grids the collider bounds (default 0.51 m), applies a sqrt-shaped submersion ramp per point, a per-point damping force proportional to mass and point velocity (factor 0.005), and raises rigidbody drag with the submerged fraction; hydrostatics alone bob like a spring. | ADAPT | 8-12 sample points per vehicle; `F = rho g Vi smooth(k)` upward at each point plus damping; buoyancy also removes wheel load, so traction drops before a truck floats. | BOATATT, HAB-buoy, GD-boat |

## 3. Terramechanics grounding

### 3.1 Formulas (units are SI-friendly; Wong's papers use kN and kPa)

| Law | Expression | Units and notes | Grade |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bekker pressure-sinkage | `p = (kc/b + kphi) * z^n` | p kPa, z m, b m (shorter patch side), kc kN/m^(n+1), kphi kN/m^(n+2), n dimensionless (Chrono: usually 0.6-1.8). For non-rectangular patches b ~ 2A/P (A area, P perimeter). In N units multiply kc, kphi by 1000. | S |
| Mohr-Coulomb strength | `tau_max = c + p*tan(phi)` | c cohesion (Pa or kPa), phi internal friction angle. | S |
| Janosi-Hanamoto shear | `tau = tau_max * (1 - exp(-j/K))` | j shear displacement (m), K shear modulus (m): ~6-12 mm firm, 25 mm loose sand/loam, 25-50 mm snow. 63% of strength at j = K. | S |
| Shear displacement, flat patch | `j = i * x` (x distance along patch) | Rigid wheel: `j = r[(th1 - th) - (1-i)(sin th1 - sin th)]`. | S |
| Slip ratio | `i = 1 - v/(r*omega)` driving; `i = r*omega/v - 1` braking | Floor the denominator at low speed; clamp friction force by `m_eff * v_slip / dt`. | S / D |
| Rigid-wheel sinkage (Wong 2012) | `z = [3W / ((3-n)(kc + b*kphi) sqrt(D))]^(2/(2n+1))` | W kN, D wheel diameter m. No slip sinkage; unreliable above z/D ~ 0.25. | S |
| Soil as a power-law spring | `Fz(z) = ((3-n)/3)(kc + b*kphi) sqrt(D) z^(n+0.5)`, stiffness `(n+0.5)*Fz/z` | Inverse of the above; no iteration needed. | D |
| Compaction resistance | `Rc = (kc + b*kphi) z^(n+1)/(n+1)`; `Rc/W = 3/((n+1)(3-n)) * sqrt(z/D)` | Diverges as n approaches 3: clamp n to 0.3-1.8. | S / D |
| Contact chord and area | `L = sqrt(D*z - z^2)`, `A = b*L` (cos th1 = 1 - z/r) | Replace by the tire's own footprint length when longer (soft tires, low pressure). | D |
| Flat-patch thrust | `H(i) = (c*A + W*tan(phi)) * [1 - (K/(i*L)) * (1 - exp(-i*L/K))]` | Saturates at `c*A + W*tan(phi)`; slip scale K/L, so mud shows large spin before grip. Integral of the Janosi law with uniform pressure. | D |
| Net traction | `F_long = H(i) - Rc - Rb - c_rr*Fz` | Drawbar pull = thrust minus compaction (Wong-Reece); we add bulldozing Rb and tire internal rolling resistance. | S / D |
| Bulldozing proxy | `Rb = b*(0.5*gamma*z^2*Kp + 2*c*z*sqrt(Kp))`, `Kp = tan^2(45 + phi/2)` | Textbook Rankine wedge; not verified in fetched sources, scale by 0.3-0.7 and gate by z/D > ~0.15. | D (unverified) |
| Elastic unloading and damping | `p_total = p_Bekker(z_plastic) + K_el*(z - z_plastic) + R*dz/dt` | Chrono: K_el (Pa/m) must exceed kphi; demo values 4e7 Pa/m and 3e4 Pa s/m. Exact combination not documented. | S / D |
| Bingham drag (deep mud) | `F = -(tau0*A_wet + mu_p*A_wet*v/h) * sign(v)` | tau0 tied to cohesion; mu_p by feel. | D / T |
| Scaling | `z ~ W^(2/(2n+1)) * D^(-1/(2n+1))`; width acts only through `b*kphi` | In cohesive mud (kc dominant) a wider tire barely helps; diameter and lower pressure help more. | D |
| Rolling resistance (hard ground) | `F = C_rr * N` | Trucks: 0.010 concrete, 0.06 medium soil, 0.25 sand (Wong 1993). | S |
| Tire-ground friction (cap thrust) | `F_max = mu*N` | Dry asphalt 0.8-0.9, wet 0.5-0.7, gravel ~0.6, earth road 0.55-0.68, hard snow 0.2, ice 0.1. Lugged rubber on firm ground: clamp thrust at mu 0.6-0.9. | S |
| Rating cone index | `RCI = CI * RI`; go if `RCI >= VCI_n` | Use only as designer-facing scalar; real VCI coefficients unverified. | S (defs only) |

### 3.3 Recommended simplified real-time per-wheel model

Run in FixedUpdate at 100 Hz (2-4 internal sub-steps for wheel spin and slip). Each step is a closed form or a few multiplies, so ~6-10 wheels x 100 Hz is trivial next to rendering.

1. **Inputs:** `Fz` from the suspension, wheel speed `omega`, ground velocity at the hub, soil parameters from the cell(s) under the footprint (blend presets by moisture/depth: log-lerp `kphi`, `kc`; lerp `n`, `c`, `phi`, `K`; scale by softness and wetness masks and cell strength S).
2. **Effective width:** `b_eff = min(b, L)`.
3. **Vertical soil force and sinkage:** `Fz_soil(z)` power-law spring plus damping; target `z_eq` from the closed form; relax `z` toward `z_eq` (first-order, no discontinuity when load changes); `z_target = z_eq * (1 + k_s * abs(i))` for slip sinkage (`k_s` ~1-2 in mud `[T]`); clamp `z < 0.8 r`. Cap contact pressure by tire inflation plus carcass stiffness `[T]`.
4. **Patch:** `L = max(sqrt(D z - z^2), tire footprint length)`, `A = b*L`.
5. **Thrust:** `Hmax = c*S*A*(1 + lug/side-wall term) + Fz*tan(phi)`; `H(i) = lug * Hmax * [1 - (K/(i L))(1 - e^(-i L/K))]` with lug factor 1.2-1.5. Lateral axis uses the slip vector `(i, tan(beta)*(1-i))`: take the magnitude through the same curve and apply it opposite to the patch sliding velocity (friction-circle behaviour, no anisotropic constants). On firm ground clamp by `mu*Fz`.
6. **Resistances:** `Rc` (closed form) + `Rb` (gated by z/D, faded inside existing ruts so rear wheels see mostly Rc) + `c_rr*Fz` + Bingham drag in deep mud and bog.
7. **Forces:** apply `(H - resistances)` and lateral force at the contact point with `AddForceAtPosition`.
8. **Write-back:** write plastic sinkage and displaced volume into the sinkage field (tread stamp, rim/berm, erosion on the dirty rectangle); only the elastic part rebounds when the wheel leaves.
9. **Strength update:** move S toward the remolded value (mud) or stiffen (sand/loam) per pass; update the rut state.
10. **Belly/axle/chassis:** separate primitives pressed into the same field so a belly-deep truck is held and accumulates drag.

Design notes: sinkage cell size should be <= b/3 (~0.08-0.12 m); ridges narrower than a cell cannot be represented. Load must feed both sinkage and shear (one-sided models feel wrong). Use the same field for visuals and forces so what the player sees is what pushes back.

### 4.2 Terrain facts

- **Heightmap resolution** is clamped to 33, 65, 129, 257, 513, 1025, 2049 or 4097; width and length 1-100,000 m; height 1-10,000 m. Sample spacing `dx = size / (res - 1)`: a 1500 m tile gives 1.465 m at 1025, 0.732 m at 2049, 0.366 m at 4097; a 1536 m tile at 2049 gives exactly 0.75 m. Memory: R16 texture `res^2 * 2` bytes (2049: 8.4 MB); a full CPU float array is `res^2 * 4`. [S][D]
- **Stored range:** heights are signed 16-bit used as unsigned; only 0..0.5 is valid (0.5 equals size.y). `SetHeights` takes 0..1, `CopyActiveRenderTextureToHeightmap` expects 0..0.5. Vertical step ~ `size.y / 32766` (3 mm at 100 m). Shaders must use `UnpackHeightmap` (on Vulkan, GLES3 and WebGPU the value is packed into two 8-bit channels). [S]
- **GPU heightmap paths:** `heightmapTexture` is a get-only RenderTexture. `DirtyHeightmapRegion` is for after you changed the GPU texture yourself; `CopyActiveRenderTextureToHeightmap` dirties internally; `TerrainHeightmapSyncControl` = None, HeightOnly, HeightAndLod. The docs never say these paths update the TerrainCollider. We skip them at runtime. [S]
- **`SetHeightsDelayLOD`** does not raise `TerrainChangedFlags.Heightmap` (listeners and caches must watch `DelayedHeightmapUpdate` or be told manually); the terrain renders at too high a LOD until `SyncHeightmap`. Arrays are `float[y,x]`, y along world Z. [S]
- **Instanced rendering:** `Terrain.drawInstanced` (default false) enables the instance renderer; per-pixel normals (`normalmapTexture`) need it. In URP's TerrainLit the vertex function `TerrainInstancing()` loads one integer heightmap texel per vertex at LOD0, so **terrain vertices cannot carry displacement finer than the heightmap spacing** (0.75 m at 2049 over 1536 m). Finer ruts need fragment-level normals/parallax or our own near-field mesh with `drawHeightmap = false`. The terrain normal map is baked from the heightmap, so sinkage normals must be added in the fragment shader. [S]
- **TerrainLit contract** (URP 17.0.4 on 6000.0/staging, 17.6 matched structurally): tags `TerrainCompatible`, Queue Geometry-100; passes ForwardLit, ShadowCaster, GBuffer, DepthOnly, DepthNormals, SceneSelectionPass, Meta; dependencies on hidden Add Pass (layers 5+, Blend One One, no shadow/depth), Base Pass (far patches) and Basemap Gen shaders. Any displacement must be mirrored in every depth and shadow pass, the Add Pass and the base-map handling; keeping 4 layers per tile avoids the Add Pass. [S]
- **Shader Graph Terrain Lit** (Unity 6.3+): Vertex Position block exists but has no effect on the far Basemap Rendering pass, so set `basemapDistance` beyond the sinkage window. We keep hand-written HLSL as the primary path. [S]
- **`Terrain.materialTemplate`** is shared (not copied): changing it affects every Terrain using that Material. Per-terrain extras use `SetSplatMaterialPropertyBlock`; world-space data uses globals. [S]
- **LOD knobs:** `heightmapPixelError`, `heightmapMaximumLOD` (1 = 1/4 triangles), `heightmapMinimumLODSimplification` (x4 per step), `basemapDistance`, `enableHeightmapLODFrustumCulling`, `patchBoundsMultiplier` (only "bounding box scale" documented; test it if sunk vertices pop). [S]
- **Layers:** URP renders four layers per pass with unlimited passes; Unity recommends <= 4 per tile. SetAlphamaps is `float[y,x,layer]`; no runtime alphamap edits for us (wetness/darkening come from the sinkage field in the shader). [S]
- **Trees and details:** use LODGroup prefabs or SpeedTree (Tree Editor is Built-in RP only); `Enable Tree Colliders` is on by default and costly; billboards get no point/spot lights. Detail meshes with GPU instancing render in batches of <= 1023, need Read/Write meshes, and do not follow sinkage, so fade them out in rutted areas. [S]
- **Queries:** `Terrain.SampleHeight` returns height relative to the terrain origin; `GetInterpolatedNormal` is a smoothed Sobel normal, not the PhysX triangle normal (two triangles per quad). Use PhysX hits for wheel contact; use the non-allocating `GetInterpolatedHeights` overload for bulk reads. `SetNeighbors` must be called on every tile (or use `allowAutoConnect` with `groupingID`). [S]
- **Map layout choice:** one 1536 m Terrain at 2049 samples is simplest for a static heightmap; 3x3 tiles of 512 m at 1025 (0.5 m) is the alternative if streaming or per-tile culling is wanted. [D]

### 4.3 Physics facts and recommended settings

- **PhysX** remains the built-in GameObject physics in 6000.6 (Project Settings > Physics has a GameObject SDK dropdown, PhysX or None). The exact PhysX build is not documented (community inference: 4.x). [S]
- **WheelCollider:** one raycast per wheel, ignores PhysicsMaterial, no width, clips on steps. Defaults (mass 20, radius 0.5, spring 35000 N/m, damper 4500 N s/m, travel 0.3 m) assume ~1500 kg; spring and damper scale linearly with mass; sprungMass is auto-computed unless set by hand. The friction model is two `WheelFrictionCurve`s (forward, sideways), each with `extremumSlip`, `extremumValue`, `asymptoteSlip`, `asymptoteValue` and a `stiffness` multiplier; the ScriptReference lists default pairs 0.2/0.4 (extremum slip), 0.5/0.8 (asymptote slip), 0.75/0.5 (asymptote value) with extremum value 1 and stiffness 1, without saying which pair belongs to which curve (usually sideways 0.2/0.5/0.75 and forward 0.4/0.8/0.5, not confirmed), and the manual page gives no slip formulas. Known Unity 6 regression (IN-80092, first seen on 6000.0.x, 6000.0.9 did not help): wheels fall through ground after Rigidbody property changes; Unity confirmed it in Aug 2024 and one user saw a fix in 6000.0.19, but other reports (Suspension Distance wrong at startup, 6000.0.23) stayed open; workaround is to disable and re-enable the WheelCollider, or reassign a value in Start; not re-verified on 6.6. [S]
- **Casts:** SphereCast skips colliders it already overlaps and its `hit.normal` is often contact-to-centre; rays starting inside a collider hit nothing; NonAlloc results are unordered and a full buffer does not guarantee the closest; `Queries Hit Triggers` is ON by default, so water or mud trigger volumes steal hits unless `QueryTriggerInteraction.Ignore` is used. `RaycastCommand` / `SpherecastCommand` batch via `QueryParameters` (results at index `N*maxHits`, valid only after the JobHandle completes); not needed for a few dozen casts per tick. [S]
- **Forces:** `AddForceAtPosition` accumulates and is consumed at the next simulation step; far application points produce large torques; apply tire and suspension forces near the hub or contact point, slightly below the centre of mass for roll control. `centerOfMass` and `inertiaTensor` set from script stop updating automatically until `ResetCenterOfMass` / `ResetInertiaTensor`; zero inertia components mean infinite inertia. [S]
- **Joints:** keep mass ratios near 2:1 (10:1 jitters); Mass Scale / Connected Mass Scale scale inverse mass (non-physical); `breakForce` acts only on Limited or Locked axes; projection is non-physical; Temporal Gauss Seidel solver handles high mass ratios better; `Rigidbody.solverIterations` overrides per body; PhysX suggests that needing more than ~30 iterations signals a bad setup. SpringJoint has a dead zone, no force cap, and distances relative to start. [S]
- **ArticulationBody:** one root, no kinematic loops (the manual points to regular joints for loops), hierarchy depth <= 64 GameObjects; the manual's stated purpose is realistic joint behaviour for commercial and industrial non-gaming uses such as robot arms; mixed Rigidbody/Articulation interaction undocumented. We skip it. [S]
- **ConfigurableJoint and SpringJoint docs:** each linear/angular axis is Free, Locked or Limited; the linear limit sets a maximum distance from the joint origin, a limit spring of zero makes the limit impassable; projection modes are None or Position and Rotation; break force/torque apply only to Limited or Locked axes (torque regardless of mode). SpringJoint: min/max distance define a range with no spring force, beyond it a spring and damper act, and the page describes no force cap. The stability page recommends 2:1 mass ratios (10:1 jitters), raising Default Solver Iterations to roughly 10-20, angular limits of at least about 5-15 degrees, and not moving joint-connected bodies via Transform. [S]
- **Timing:** `Time.fixedDeltaTime` default 0.02; `Time.maximumDeltaTime` default 0.3333 means up to ~16 catch-up steps at the default 0.02 s and ~33 at 0.01 s (a spiral-of-doom risk). `Physics.Simulate` steps above 0.03 are inaccurate; `FixedUpdate` keeps its own rate in Script mode. PhysX vehicle stability rule for suspension springs: `alpha = sqrt(sprungMass/stiffness)/timestep`, which equals `1/(omega*dt)`; the PhysX doc asks for alpha above 5 and calls alpha near 1 almost certainly unstable, and notes the exact threshold is hard to predict; its stiffness guidance is `stiffness = naturalFrequency^2 * sprungMass`. [S]
- **CCD:** Discrete is default and enough (vehicle moves <= 0.25 m per 0.01 s step); Continuous Speculative can create ghost contacts; sweep CCD ignores rotation. [S]
- **Unity 6.6 API drift:** `Component.rigidbody` removed; int instance IDs replaced by `EntityId` (`RaycastHit.colliderEntityId`); Rigidbody uses `linearVelocity`, `linearDamping`, `angularDamping`; `Physics.autoSyncTransforms` deprecated. Code against the new names from day one. [S]

### 4.4 Shader / URP facts (verified on this machine unless marked)

- **Four-pass custom lit shader compiles and renders in URP 17.6:** ForwardLit (`UniversalForward`), ShadowCaster, DepthOnly, DepthNormals, each with `multi_compile_instancing` and `DOTS.hlsl`. One shared displacement function is mandatory: every pass must output identical positions or depth priming, SSAO and DBuffer decals misrender and shadows detach from sunken wheels. A render test moved geometry 15 px for -0.5 m where 16 px was expected (rasterisation rounding). [E]
- **SRP Batcher:** all Properties-block values live in one `UnityPerMaterial` CBUFFER, identical in every pass (put it in `HLSLINCLUDE`); project-wide data (mud window texture and float4s) is declared outside any CBUFFER and fed by `Shader.SetGlobal*`. `ShaderUtil.GetSRPBatcherCompatibilityCode` returned 0 (compatible) after a render (it reads "not initialized" before the first render). `MaterialPropertyBlock` breaks both SRP Batcher and the GPU Resident Drawer. [E][S]
- **Vertex texture sampling:** use `SAMPLE_TEXTURE2D_LOD`; the shader-model table says target 2.0 has no vertex texture sampling, so use `#pragma target 3.5` in all four passes (target 2.0 still rendered on this D3D12 desktop, so the rule is portability-relevant only). [E][S]
- **Toroidal sinkage window:** one RHalf texture with Repeat wrap, sampled by absolute world XZ (`uv = xz / W`), so the CPU rewrites only scrolled rows/columns (`index = ((cell mod N) + N) mod N`); a float4 global carries window centre for an edge fade. Store displacement relative to terrain (RHalf has ~3 decimal digits). [E]
- **Normals:** central differences (4 extra taps) in the vertex stage, applied identically in DepthNormals; per-pixel version only if the mesh is coarser than the field. Shader Graph's Normal From Texture is a forward difference with a UV-space offset, not metric. [S]
- **Forward+:** needs `_CLUSTER_LIGHT_LOOP`, normalized screen-space UV in `InputData`, and a local named `inputData` for the light-loop macros; simplest path is to build InputData/SurfaceData and call `UniversalFragmentPBR`. [S]
- **Triplanar and layers:** Shader Graph Triplanar node is fragment-only (weights = normalized `pow(abs(N), k)`, three planar samples); hand-write it as HLSL. A `Texture2DArray` (create in code, fill with `Graphics.CopyTexture`, `SAMPLE_TEXTURE2D_ARRAY`, target 3.5, no Crunch) lets one material carry mud, dirt, gravel, grass and rock without the 4-layer limit. [S]
- **Water/refraction:** Opaque Texture and Depth Texture checkboxes (already on in `PC_RPAsset`); `SampleSceneColor`, `SampleSceneDepth`, `ComputeWorldSpacePosition(uv, depth, UNITY_MATRIX_I_VP)`; the water material must draw after the snapshot (transparent or queue 2999+). Shader Graph Scene Color/Depth are fragment-only. [S]
- **Decals:** Decal Projector needs a Decal Shader Graph material, cannot use SRP Batcher, DBuffer mode needs the DepthNormal prepass: good for rare marks, not per-wheel ruts. [S]
- **Instancing:** `Graphics.RenderMeshInstanced` caps at 1023 instances per call (511 with `worldToObject`; drop it with `assumeuniformscaling`) and needs `enableInstancing`; `RenderMeshIndirect` needs compute support. GPU Resident Drawer needs Forward+, no MPB, DOTS-instanced shaders, <= 128 materials; keep it off at first but include the DOTS lines now. [S]
- **Render Graph:** URP 17 uses it; avoid custom ScriptableRenderPasses (plain shader passes and URP toggles are enough). [S]

CPU-to-GPU upload patterns measured on the RTX 5070, main-thread cost only, no frame pacing `[E]`:

| Method | 256^2 | 512^2 | 1024^2 | 2048^2 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `Texture2D.Apply` (RHalf, updateMipmaps false) | 0.018 ms | 0.20 ms | 1.15 ms | 2.61 ms |
| `SetPixelData` + Apply | 0.11 ms | 0.47 ms | 1.44 ms | 5.36 ms |
| 64x64 tile Apply + `Graphics.CopyTexture` into a 2048^2 RenderTexture | ~0.025 ms per tile (verified by readback) | | | |

Conclusion: dirty-tile copies make per-frame cost proportional to wheel footprint (20 tiles ~0.5 ms); a full 1024^2 Apply would burn ~7% of a 60 fps frame. Write through `GetRawTextureData<ushort>` (aliases CPU memory, no allocation) from Burst jobs; use a non-readable RenderTexture as the copy destination and never Apply it. `AsyncGPUReadback` is supported but we do not read back into physics.

### 4.5 Burst, Jobs, Collections, Mathematics

- In 6000.6 Burst is an engine module (the `com.unity.burst` 2.0.0 package is a type-forwarder shell); Unity.Mathematics is the `MathematicsModule` engine module; Unity.Jobs lives in the engine; `com.unity.collections` 6.6.0 is a core package pinned to the Editor. **Do not add burst or mathematics to `Packages/manifest.json`.** [E][S]

In Play mode Burst compiles asynchronously by default (code may run as JIT-compiled managed code meanwhile); `CompileSynchronously = true` forces native code and waits, which can hitch the frame, so use it in tests and profiling runs and discard the first (warm-up) timing; builds compile ahead of time. [S]

Batch size: start at 1 and raise until gains vanish (tile jobs: 1-8 tiles per batch). Plan: tire/sinkage patch update as one Burst job over the active tiles, writing through `GetRawTextureData<ushort>`; suspension and rigidbody forces stay on the main thread (PhysX objects are not job-safe). [S][T]

## 5. Decisions

### 5.1 Ground representation

**Decision:** smooth static Unity Terrain + TerrainCollider (physical surface) + a project-owned virtual sinkage field (CPU truth) + GPU vertex displacement (visual).

- **Physical surface:** one 1536 m Terrain, heightmap 2049 (0.75 m); never written at runtime. Wheel casts hit the PhysX terrain; the sinkage field is subtracted on our side. Gives deterministic hits and no collider rebuilds.
- **Sinkage field:** 16 m tiles, 128 x 128 cells (12.5 cm, the resolution proven by MudRunner), stored as signed deltas relative to terrain, allocated on first wheel contact; plus per-cell strength S (remold/compaction), hidden max depth, softness and wetness masks. Toroidal active window (start 256 m = 2048^2 cells, 8 MB at 16-bit) feeds a Repeat-wrapped RHalf texture; dirty 64x64 tiles go up with `Graphics.CopyTexture`.
- **Visual:** phase A uses a forked, project-owned TerrainLit (via `materialTemplate`, `drawInstanced` on) with vertex displacement mirrored in all passes. Because terrain vertices are 0.75 m apart, phase B is the escape hatch: `drawHeightmap = false` plus a camera-following clipmap mesh (finest ring ~12 cm over 64-100 m, coarser rings out to the map edge) sampling the terrain heightmap and the sinkage texture, with `basemapDistance` beyond the window. Decide by looking at ruts in the proving ground.
- **Conflicts resolved:** tile size 16 m (not 24 m) because it matches the proven 128 x 128 mud resolution; 1536 = 96 tiles.
- **Persistence:** only touched tiles, 16-bit deltas, caps on depth/berm height, optional settle pass.

### 5.2 Suspension

**Decision:** project-owned per-wheel spherecast/raycast suspension on a plain Rigidbody; no WheelCollider, no ArticulationBody.

Justification: WheelCollider is a closed slip model with no width or mud hook and had a Unity 6 regression; its defaults are tuned for 1500 kg while we need 3-12 t. We need per-wheel sinkage, load and slip exposed to the mud module, which a custom model gives directly. ArticulationBody is robotics-oriented, restricts loops (rope between vehicles), and its mixing with Rigidbodies is undocumented. Implementation rules: FixedUpdate at 100 Hz; spherecast (radius = tire radius minus softness) for distance, short ray or terrain normal for the true normal; `QueryTriggerInteraction.Ignore`; NonAlloc buffers; `k` from target frequency and per-wheel sprung mass recomputed from CoM; forces near the hub, slightly below CoM; solid-axle coupling limiting left/right travel difference; speed-floored slip and force clamps; wheel spin integrated with sub-steps.

### 5.3 Tire / mud model summary

Per wheel: soil spring (Bekker closed form) with damping and an unloading branch; relaxation of z toward equilibrium plus slip sinkage; contact patch from sinkage and tire footprint; Janosi-limited thrust on a flat patch with lug factor and a combined-slip vector; resistances Rc + Rb + rolling + Bingham drag; write-back of plastic sinkage, rim/berm and erosion into the field; strength S evolves per pass. Parameters come from SurfaceSO rows (section 3.4), blended per cell by softness and wetness, with `TireSO` multipliers (lug, void ratio, width, diameter, pressure). Mass raises both sinkage and grip. Everything beyond the formula skeleton is tuned in the proving ground.

### 5.4 Drivetrain

Real ratio chain (engine torque curve, gearbox, transfer low/high, axle) with derived per-gear speeds shown in the UI and an optional wheel-speed clamp; engine stall below stall rpm under load; per-axle open/locked differentials and a centre/transfer state with AWD/diff-lock availability flags; engine and gearbox damage curves; fuel from BSFC x power x multipliers with a game-time scale. Conflict resolved: SnowRunner's speed-cap gearbox is NOT copied as a physical model.

### 5.5 Water

Baked water height and flow grids; multi-point buoyancy (8-12 points), current drag, intake/snorkel drowning point, per-body fluid flag, URP refraction shader; optional ping-pong wake texture as stretch.

### 5.6 Winch and trailers

Winch = custom unilateral velocity-level rope constraint (effective-mass impulse capped by `Fcap*dt`, scalar rope length, Verlet visual). A ConfigurableJoint distance limit (X, Y, Z Limited, zero limit spring) is kept as a comparison baseline in the proving ground; SpringJoint is not used. Trailers = ConfigurableJoint hitch (limits in section 2.5), raised per-body solver iterations, `massScale` when the ratio exceeds ~4:1, uncouple/re-couple. Conflict resolved: the two Unity-physics research passes differed on rope implementation; we implement the custom constraint first and compare.

### 5.7 Tooling

Unity CLI wrapper plus raw `Unity.exe` fallback; EditMode/PlayMode tests with the asmdef layout above; screenshots via camera readback without `-nographics`; short project path; Blender through the localhost:9891 MCP.

### 5.8 Reconciled conflicts and open choices
